BWP and marine plywood

Marine plywood is the highest grade of plywood: every ply is a selected hardwood, bonded with phenol-formaldehyde resin and treated against fungus and borers. It is made to IS 710 and passes the 72-hour boiling water test. BWP plywood to IS 303 uses the same resin with a wider choice of core species.

Where it is used

  • Boat building and jetties
  • Bus, truck and container bodies
  • Exterior cladding and signboards
  • Bathrooms, wet kitchens and coastal construction

Marine plywood is not needed for dry interiors; MR or BWR does the same job there at a much lower price.

What we check before loading

  • IS 710 marine and IS 303 BWP are different standards; we quote the one you specify and print it on the invoice.
  • Look for solid cores with no gaps when the sheet is cut.
  • For boats, ask for gurjan face and core, and edge-seal after cutting.

Questions buyers ask

What is the difference between BWP and marine?

Both use waterproof phenolic resin. Marine (IS 710) also specifies the wood species, a gap-free core and preservative treatment, so it is stronger and more consistent.

Does marine plywood need paint?

For exterior use, yes. Seal all edges and coat with an exterior primer and paint or varnish.
